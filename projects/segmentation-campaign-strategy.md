# Segmentation & Campaign Strategy

## Why Segmentation Matters

A subscriber list is not one audience.

Different customers can have different levels of engagement, purchase history and intent. Segmentation allows the message to reflect those differences.

## Example Segments

### Engaged Subscribers

Profiles who recently opened or clicked emails.

**Use case:** New products, educational content and promotional campaigns.

### Non-Purchasers

Subscribers who have not placed an order.

**Use case:** Brand education, product value and first-purchase incentives.

### Recent Customers

Customers who purchased recently.

**Use case:** Post-purchase education, complementary products and retention messaging.

### Repeat Customers

Customers with multiple purchases.

**Use case:** Loyalty-focused communication, new product announcements and early-access campaigns.

### At-Risk Customers

Previously active customers who have not purchased within the expected repeat-purchase period.

**Use case:** Reactivation and winback campaigns.

## Campaign Planning Process

1. Define the campaign objective.
2. Identify the audience.
3. Check relevant customer behavior.
4. Build the segment.
5. Develop the message and offer.
6. Review subject line, content and CTA.
7. Test the email.
8. Schedule or send.
9. Monitor performance.
10. Use the results to inform the next campaign.

## Example

If a subscriber completed the Welcome Flow, engaged with the emails, but still did not purchase, I would not automatically create another flow just because they did not convert.

Instead, I could create a targeted campaign for an appropriate non-purchaser segment, such as:

**Brand story / trust-building message → product value → relevant offer**

This keeps automation focused on lifecycle events while campaigns can address broader marketing objectives.
