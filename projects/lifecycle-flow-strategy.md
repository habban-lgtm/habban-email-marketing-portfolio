# Lifecycle Flow Strategy

## Objective

Create automated communication around meaningful customer behaviors instead of sending the same message to every subscriber.

## Customer Lifecycle

```text
Visitor
   ↓
Subscriber
   ↓
Welcome
   ↓
Product Consideration
   ↓
Purchase
   ↓
Repeat Purchase
   ↓
Reactivation
```

## Core Flow Map

| Lifecycle Stage | Customer Signal | Automation |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Subscribes | Welcome Flow |
| Consideration | Checkout started | Abandoned Checkout |
| Purchase intent | Product added to cart | Abandoned Cart |
| Retention | Customer purchased | Post-purchase / retention messaging |
| Reactivation | Customer becomes inactive | Winback |
| List hygiene | Long-term inactivity | Sunset |

## How I Decide Between a Flow and a Campaign

### Flow

I use a flow when the message should happen because of a specific customer action or condition.

Examples:
- Subscribed
- Started checkout
- Added to cart
- Purchased
- Became inactive

### Campaign

I use a campaign when I want to communicate with a selected audience at a specific time.

Examples:
- Product launch
- Seasonal promotion
- Brand story
- Educational content
- Limited-time offer

## Optimization Mindset

After launch, I look at the relevant performance signals and ask:

- Are customers entering the flow as expected?
- Are emails being delivered successfully?
- Are customers engaging with the message?
- Are clicks leading to meaningful site actions?
- Are different segments behaving differently?
- Is the sequence too long, too short, or poorly timed?
- Are inactive profiles affecting list quality?

The goal is continuous improvement rather than simply launching an automation and leaving it unchanged.
